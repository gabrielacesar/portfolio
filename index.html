2020

App Discovery: Luccas Toon

game designer ux designer ux researcher

A project by 01 Digital to deliver an entertainment and gaming app for client Luccas Neto.

📋 Executive Summary

01 Digital was hired by YouTuber Luccas Neto to design his first app from scratch. Faced with YouTube's changing monetization policies for children's content, the client sought to diversify his revenue streams. The project followed a full discovery-to-delivery process — from user research with children and guardians, through ideation workshops, rapid prototyping, and usability testing — resulting in an app that surpassed 600,000 downloads in its first month on the market.

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Team & Roles

  • Design Lead • André Roberti
  • UX Designer and Researcher • Gabriela Cesar
  • Game Designer • Gabriela Cesar
  • UI Designer and Illustrations • Hector Paoletti
  • UI Designer and Illustrations • Juliana Iasi
  • Design Assistant • Renata Caldas

📖 Context

YouTuber Luccas Neto hired 01 Digital to build his first app entirely from scratch! At the time (2019–20), YouTube had just changed its monetization policy for children's videos, impacting the creator and his sources of income. As a result, Luccas sought to expand his product lineup by investing in a dedicated app.

🗺️ Project Phases

Understanding

Problem investigation, gathering technical and content requirements, hypotheses, benchmarking.

Prototyping

Based on the data we gathered, how can we create a rapid prototype to test our hypotheses?

Pre-launch

MVP version of the app. Usability testing with the app in production. Roadmap for improvements.

🔍 Discovery & Problem Framing

How we began to understand which of people's problems the app could address

Needs Assessment

We analyzed hundreds of comments posted on Luccas Neto's YouTube channel, and after careful analysis, we identified some key needs:

  • closer contact with Luccas, to receive individual attention
  • learning more about Luccas, to be part of the Aventureiros

Understanding the children's needs was the starting point for deciding what the new product should address and what its main objective would be, from the end-user's perspective.

However, no business can sustain itself by merely addressing the customer's needs alone. Therefore, we moved on to a phase of understanding the needs not only of the customer but also of secondary users (guardians, for example) and the available technology.

Product Requirements

  • Consider the creator economy as a business requirement

  • Unity development for quicker deployment

  • Parents must be able to control what the children access

  • Audience: children from 3 to 11 years old

Since this is a children's app, it needed to cater to two distinct audiences, each with its own characteristics.

Adults (guardians), who seek: control over the content viewed; trust in the technology; a safe and customizable environment. Source: previous research on the Masha and the Bear app.

Children (users) belonging to the following audiences: from the YouTube channel (ages 3–11); already consume Luccas Neto's content; use other mobile apps and games.

In addition, we also identified the following requirements that the product must meet:

  • Client: have its own monetization platform

  • Technical: an app developed in Unity to enable scalable game implementation

  • Accessibility: cater to children at different stages of literacy

Ideation

The 01 Digital design team brought together representatives from various business and technology areas to brainstorm which product features could be created to meet the needs of its users. The ideation process took place in three stages:

  • Crazy 8 – a workshop tool that allowed the team to generate as many ideas as possible within a limited timeframe.
  • Clustering – grouping proposals into categories based on similarity
  • Value vs. Complexity Matrix – categorizing features based on ease of implementation and value delivered to the end user

The ideas selected to move on to the next stage (prototyping) had to be directly related to addressing the user needs identified earlier.

The collaborative ideation session also allowed the various departments at 01 Digital to articulate their needs and goals regarding the app, as well as to voice their limitations and concerns. It was an excellent opportunity for everyone to take ownership of the project and for us to better understand the internal stakeholders' expectations for the product.

Benchmarking

Research on similar apps and games to help visualize the solution

In addition to the ideation phase, before moving on to the final solution, it was important to research the market for children's games and apps that address similar problems, even if they target different audiences.

Benchmarking Results

DC Super Hero Girls Blitz Budge

  • Adventure World Immersion — A game-like visual experience applied even to routine tasks — navigating app sections and completing control activities feels like gameplay.
  • Infinite Explorable Environment — An open-ended world design that leaves room for future updates, seasonal content, and special decorations unlocked through achievements or easter eggs.

PK XD Playkids

  • Multi-purpose Avatar — The avatar serves multiple functions within the interface: representing the player during activities, enabling communication with other users, and powering an in-app coin economy.
  • Character Empathy as Motivation — Emotional connection to the characters sparks children's desire to become adventurers themselves. Previous research showed kids already identify their character's color, power, and custom outfit preferences.

Brawl Stars Supercell

  • In-app Purchases via Earned Currency — Certain app content (videos, avatar items, in-game resources such as hints) can be purchased using coins earned through gameplay — keeping spending tied to effort.
  • Achievement System — Achievements are merit-based rewards granted when players reach a milestone within the app. Upon completion, new items are unlocked or players receive coin rewards.

Cube Escape Rusty Lake

  • Mystery-driven Exploration — Players are encouraged to explore an enigmatic environment, discovering items that help them progress — driving curiosity and continued engagement.
  • Luccas Neto Universe Integration — Opportunity to incorporate characters and villains from the Luccas Neto franchise, deepening brand connection.
  • Escape Room Adventure Format — The escape room mechanic is a natural fit — Luccas Neto has already explored this format in his YouTube content (e.g., the "Trapped in the Toy Room" video), giving it built-in recognition with the audience.

LinkedIn Reactions LinkedIn

  • Emotional Reactions to Videos — Beyond watching, children can express how a video made them feel and view other users' reactions — adding a social and emotional layer to content consumption.
  • Mood-based Video Discovery — Video ratings feed into personalized search filters (e.g., videos to laugh to, music videos), helping kids find content that matches their current mood.

BTS World Netmarble

  • Simulated Call Interface — The app mimics a phone call UI, creating the feeling that Luccas is speaking directly to the child — a parasocial interaction made interactive.
  • Native Creator Voice — Luccas Neto already uses a direct, child-facing communication style in his YouTube videos, making the transition to in-app interaction feel authentic and familiar.

💡 Solution & Delivery

Time to roll up our sleeves and put the product to the test!

To optimize our time, we then set out to define an MVP that would allow the product to deliver value with minimal technical effort. After agreeing with the client (Luccas Neto) on the scope of features to be active at the app's launch, we began planning the prototyping and testing phases.

To test the MVP concepts, the team devised several strategies to conduct the tests as quickly as possible and with the least possible use of resources:

  • Avatar: paper dolls.

  • Interaction with Luccas: a paper letter for children to write.

  • Super Player: a simple HTML version.

  • Escape Room: a navigable prototype of the game in Figma.

  • Profile (Achievements) and Signature: static wireframe in Figma.

In a pre-pandemic context (late 2019), we conducted in-person testing at 01 Digital's headquarters with children aged 5 to 9 who are fans of Luccas Neto, accompanied by their guardians.

Concept Test

Avatar

The children experimented with colors on a paper doll. They see themselves as Adventurers—they already know their power, color, and even their "transformation"! In addition, they wrote briefly about their favorite videos and what message they'd like to send to their idol, Luccas Neto.

Super Player

Using a medium-fidelity prototype (HTML), we were able to simulate the Super Player feature (a video player with real-time reactions). The children show their reactions to the videos, customizing their experience in the app—comedy (funny), scare, etc.

Escape Room Game

Using a medium-fidelity prototype (Figma), we were able to simulate the Escape Room game. The children really loved the game! They thought it was fun that the "reactions" to the objects in the room were "narrated" by Luccas.

Point to Note: Pre-literate children rely exclusively on icons and audio: the final version of the game must be accessible to this audience.

In-Game Economy

Using a low-fidelity prototype (Figma), we were able to assess how children and their mothers perceived the app's Achievements and economy features. The use of coins and the acquisition of new items serve as a constant incentive and challenge to keep children engaged.

When asked, the mothers reported that the Premium mode seems advantageous because of the extra coins, but that they would initially use the free mode to test the app. They also mentioned that the presence of ads in the videos isn't a problem, since the children are already used to them.

Approval of the look and feel with the client

Applying insights from the tests and moving on to the development phase with client approval

After the tests of the main features with the children yielded mostly very positive results, we moved on to the app's first UI and client approval. In this stage, we demonstrated the app's most important workflows in a simplified manner.

Onboarding

After the splash screen, the child is directed to a video featuring a mockup UI of a call from Luccas speaking directly to them in a landscape video. Suggested maximum duration: 1 minute.

After that, the app's control panel will be "turned off" until the child chooses one of the new Power Stones and begins to customize their experience.

Avatar

With the control panel "activated," it's time to customize your adventurer and start exploring the LuccasToon app! Customization options include accessories and special items unlocked through Achievements. The child can acquire items using coins, by watching ads, or through purchases in the App Stores.

Selling outfits for the avatar was a completely new monetization model for 01 Digital at the time.

Home and Videos

On the app's home screen, children can choose between games, view their profile, and watch videos. All of this is done in a playful way, without losing sight of the goal of showcasing the app's features. Among the videos, children can choose their favorite type or search for ones they want to watch again.

Once the research and approval stages with the client are complete, the app moves on to the UI design for all the flows to be included in the launch version and code implementation by the developers.

Pre-launch

Beta testing of the app during the pre-launch phase

Goals and requirement: detect usability issues; remote and moderate testing (pandemic); children between 5 to 11 years old; exclusive for Luccas Neto fans.

Goals of the beta test: analyze the overall user experience of the app (the first time we present the complete app to children); verify fixes made during internal quality testing; identify usability issues to be fixed before launch; insights and improvements for the future.

Due to the COVID-19 pandemic, testing for this phase was conducted 100% remotely using the Lookback tool.

Key results from the pre-launch test

Onboarding

The younger children were very excited because they thought it was a real call from Luccas Neto. All children: extremely positive reaction. They retain the information Luccas shared (they pay close attention). Potential opportunity for future videos introducing app features or campaigns.

Videos

Use of horizontal navigation to search for videos. Children did not use the "My Videos" tab. Search optimization: Children use it as if it were the YouTube search (they typed "Luccas Neto," for example). They didn't use Reacts spontaneously, but reacted positively when asked to try them.

Avatar

Main uses: creating characters that already exist in the Luccas Toon universe, and creating a version of themselves in the app. Difficulty selecting clothing colors (touch area) and removing accessories. The share button was not identified. Switching stones: the flow is not very intuitive, except when the child is already navigating within the Avatar section.

Escape Room

The drawing on the wall and the placement of symbols as "hints" confused the children: they expect the solution to be placing the items exactly as shown in the drawing. Difficulty with some touch areas and the drag gesture. Children kept trying to interact with and solve puzzles they had already completed. It's necessary to indicate that those tasks are already finished.

Overall, this was the children's favorite part of the entire app.

Some lessons we learned from this research

  • According to Lookback, at the time of the test (2020), iOS devices did not allow screen sharing and front-facing camera use simultaneously, leaving us unable to see users' reactions in real time
  • We asked children to wear headphones during the test and to position themselves close to the Wi-Fi signal
  • The "video game controller" icon in the main menu proved very effective at communicating that this section contains games
  • Explore the Home button tap gesture for navigating between islands—we had been using the drag gesture, which proved to be unintuitive during the test

Although it wasn't the primary objective, the test also raised some questions about the monetization model, possibilities for evolving existing features, and suggestions for new features from the children:

  • The children have already watched all of Luccas Neto's videos on other streaming platforms. What will be our unique selling point when selling Luccas's videos through our own app?
  • While browsing, the children look for free alternatives or stop using a feature when they realize it's a paid one
  • Suggestions for more features "using" the Avatar character they created; online/collaborative games and activities with slime and dinosaurs

Insights for our team and process improvements

What worked well, and what would we do differently today?

After the app's final delivery and release in app stores (Android and iOS devices), we can say there are positives as well as opportunities for improvement:

  • Conducting remote testing is quite challenging when working with mobile products and child users.
  • Since the app was already published in beta, it required more effort from the tutors to install the app and conduct the tests.
  • We regret not having documented our processes better, as we had difficulty reviewing previous stages and decision-making processes.
  • We had over 600,000 downloads in the first month after the app's release (see chart on the right). This demonstrates a clear alignment between the user's problem and the proposed solution.
  • We were also fortunate to have a client who was a true partner in our initiative, as YouTuber Luccas Neto collaborated creatively on the app, providing assets for the games (e.g., games and videos) and his entire visual identity kit to support our design team. In addition, Luccas himself promoted the app extensively on his channel, including a launch campaign.
  • Another notable positive aspect was the collaboration among various teams at 01 Digital, such as Marketing, Development, and Design. Without the sense that this project was the result of collective effort and cooperation, we wouldn't have achieved such a positive outcome in such a short time.

Roadmap and Improvements

Next Steps After Launch

Immediately following the launch of the Luccas Toon app, several issues and opportunities were identified:

  • Monetization – "packs" of outfits and accessories for the avatar were one of the app's major sources of revenue (see chart on the right). How can we maintain this model's long-term sustainability?
  • Pre-developed games – Create games set in the Luccas Neto universe, leveraging ready-made Unity engines (e.g., Candy Crush). However, how can we determine whether the app's users are the target audience for such games?
  • Creative collaboration – Client Luccas Neto hired 01 Digital to develop a second app, this time focused on preteens. How can we keep the client satisfied and create another app that follows the successful model?
  • Subscription Benefits – Reviewing, together with the client, the best benefits for subscribers to the app's content, given that much of the content is available for free on YouTube.

Currently, 01 Digital continues as the official publisher of the Luccas Toon app and uses the research and design thinking process employed in this project as a model for other consulting firms and clients—a proven approach to applying research and creating user-centered experiences.

📈 Results & Learnings

  • 600,000+ downloads in the first month after release on Android and iOS — demonstrating clear alignment between the identified user problem and the proposed solution.

  • Avatar outfit and accessory packs became one of the app's major revenue sources, establishing a new monetization model for 01 Digital.

  • Process adopted as a reference model by 01 Digital for other consulting engagements — the research and design thinking approach from this project became a template for future user-centered product work at the company.

Soybean storage silo declaration ticket

product designer

Redesign of the Soybean storage silo declaration ticket in Conexão Biotec, a management portal for soybean crops containing valid-patent biotechnology.

📋 Executive Summary

Redesign of the Grain Storage (Silo) Declaration ticket flow in Conexão Biotec — a portal used by the Brazilian soybean industry to manage royalty payments. The project addressed pain points on both sides of the workflow: farmers opening tickets and analysts reviewing them. Working against a tight seasonal deadline, discovery, ideation, and prototyping were delivered within a single quarter, with no formal usability testing due to time constraints.

🗺️ Project Phases

Discovery

Field research and interviews to identify pain points on both the farmer and analyst sides of the ticket workflow.

Ideation

Cross-functional workshop with analysts, auditors, and business teams to co-design solutions for the most complex pain points.

Prototyping

Solution design for both ticket opening (farmer flow) and ticket analysis (internal team flow), delivered against a June deadline ahead of the September soybean season.

📖 Context

In Brazil, farmers pay royalties to companies that develop genetically modified soybean seeds resistant to specific pests or herbicides, increasing productivity and reducing costs. These payments are proportional to the area planted with modified seeds, regulated by contracts between farmers and biotechnology companies. This system is crucial for financing research and development of new soybean varieties, promoting a continuous cycle of innovation in the Brazilian agricultural sector.

The Conexão Biotec portal, maintained by the soy industry, is an essential tool to facilitate these royalty payments. It supports Bayer, Corteva, BASF, and Syngenta industries in efficiently collecting these payments. Bayer is responsible for the portal's operation, ensuring transparent information and agility in responding to farmers regarding royalty payments and volume release at distribution points.

🔍 Discovery & Problem Framing

Pain Points Overview

One of the second-quarter 2024 priorities for the Conexão Biotec portal was improving the flow for opening and analyzing Grain Storage (Silo) Declaration tickets. The main reason is the slowness in handling these tickets, which can take 8 to 14 days for full resolution, even though they represent less than 30% of the portal's total ticket volume.

Since soybean season in Brazil typically begins around September–October, it was determined that improvements should be delivered by the Design team by the end of June, so development could begin in July, with subsequent testing and production release in September.

Soybean Grain Storage

If a farmer mixes patented biotech soybeans with conventional soybeans, they may face legal issues and fines when delivering the soybeans to a distribution point, since patented biotech soybeans have specific usage rules. However, storing soybeans in privately owned silos helps the farmer maintain grain quality, protect against pests, save on transportation costs, and hold the harvest until prices improve.

With this conflict in mind, the Conexão Biotec portal allows farmers to open a ticket declaring the volume in kilograms of mixed soybeans stored in the silo and, in doing so, obtain a release of that amount for delivery at distribution points — provided the royalties due were already paid at the time of seed purchase during planting.

Farmer Pain Points [1]

  • The seasonality of opening and closing the Grain Storage ticket is not clear

  • The main prerequisite of the ticket must be made explicit: the farmer must own a private silo

  • Allow review of ticket data before actually submitting it, letting the user go back and correct any information if needed

  • Make it clear that the comments field can be used to report planting conditions that affect land productivity (artificial irrigation, row spacing, etc.) to assist in ticket handling

  • Indicate the possibility of a crop audit, signaling the chance of contact via calls and WhatsApp messages

Ticket Analysis Pain Points [2]

  • Bulk action on document analysis is not possible for supporting documents attached to tickets. There are cases with more than 50 documents, requiring each one to be approved/rejected individually

  • Evaluate the possibility of attaching files larger than 7 MB to the ticket, since crop audit reports can exceed 30 MB

  • Invoice declarations expressed in seeds (big bags and 140,000-seed bags) conflict with the portal's kilogram-only system, creating obstacles in area calculations and prolonging ticket analysis

[1] Identified in previous research conducted by MJV and Accenture consultancies in the field, as well as complaints submitted through the Conexão Biotec portal / WhatsApp channel

[2] Analysts, reviewers, and auditors of Storage tickets were interviewed directly by me between April and May 2024, in semi-structured interviews conducted online via Microsoft Teams

💡 Solution & Delivery

Solution development took place in two phases: (1) Ticket Opening [addressing Farmer pain points] and (2) Ticket Analysis [addressing analyst, reviewer, and auditor pain points]. Given the delivery deadline, the solution presented was evaluated by the commercial analysis team and ticket attendants themselves (the people closest to farmers, who directly receive their complaints on a daily basis), since there was no time to conduct formal usability testing.

Ticket Opening

Pain Point 1

The seasonality of opening and closing the ticket is not clear.

To make it clear that the Storage ticket has variable seasonality and to list the documents required for opening it, an onboarding screen was implemented where this information is presented before the flow even begins.

Pain Point 2

It is not clear that a private silo is a prerequisite for the ticket.

Although in many cases dialog windows create unnecessary friction in web systems, this component was introduced here precisely to confirm the ticket's basic premise — without which all requests will be canceled: the farmer must mandatorily own a private silo.

Point raised while investigating common reasons for canceled tickets: User opens a ticket for soybean varieties for which they have no active licensing.

One of the most common reasons for rejected tickets is the lack of a soybean biotechnology licensing agreement. To make this clearer during the opening process, a warning tooltip was added when the farmer is declaring the soybean varieties present in their silo, flagging any missing licenses.

Pain Points 3 and 4

Allow review of ticket data and clarify the function of the Comments field.

To assist with reviewing ticket data and prevent submissions with incorrect information, a new information review step will help farmers revisit all previously filled data and, if needed, edit anything that is incorrect.

In the Comments section of this screen, placeholder text was added to the input field with instructions on how to best use this component: "If applicable, report planting conditions that affect technology productivity (artificial irrigation, etc.)."

This way, exceptional cases requiring differentiated audit handling are flagged at the ticket opening stage.

Pain Point 5

Signal the need for contact when a ticket requires an on-site auditor.

To inform the farmer that they must be available for audit scheduling attempts, we added the following alert message to the ticket tracking view at the corresponding step:

ℹ️ Crop Monitoring — The team of independent assessors will reach out using the contact details registered on the portal (WhatsApp, email, and phone). Facilitating the on-site inspection helps speed up your ticket analysis.

A sensitive aspect of this step is avoiding panic in the user upon learning their ticket is undergoing an audit. For this reason, we use the words monitoring and inspection and emphasize that allowing this visit helps resolve the ticket more quickly.

Ticket Analysis

Pain Point 1

It is not possible to approve or reject attached ticket documents in bulk.

The previous version of the Attached Documents module required the analyst to individually select a decision for each document. In the new proposal, by introducing a checkbox with parent-child behavior, the analyst can select all documents in the ticket at once and perform bulk actions based on their selection.

Pain Point 2

It is not possible to attach documents larger than 7 MB, such as audit reports.

After careful analysis with the PO and back-end development team, the task of verifying file transfer and storage capacity was left as a backlog item for the technical team, as it depends on the bandwidth capacity contracted with the portal's provider.

Pain Point 3

Allow volume entry in seeds during ticket analysis.

This was the most complex task, as the area calculation derived from the farmer's declared volume depends on a series of agronomic indices that are calculated differently depending on whether the volume is expressed in seeds or kilograms:

  • For area calculation from kilograms of harvested soybeans, the system must have the correct TSW (thousand-seed weight) for the soybean variety declared in the ticket, along with the relative plantability index (bags per hectare). Both indices can be retrieved by the system from the soybean industry itself, but are subject to variance depending on natural factors or the specifics of each soybean seed lot.
  • For area calculation from number of soybean seeds, the system requires the expected population index (plants per hectare) for that seed.

As seen in the old version of the system (see image below), the analyst made their assessment using only kilogram and variety information, with the system automatically populating Plantability and Area fields.

Due to the high complexity and interdependency of the new data to be added to the ticket, a collective ideation workshop was held with the business team, auditors, reviewers, and analysts. Cross-functional teams mapped the success requirements for redesigning this stage of the ticket, along with a low-fidelity prototyping exercise ("sketch frame") to address the identified needs.

Invoice Declaration Requirements:

  • User must choose whether to declare data in kilograms or seeds

  • For kilograms, the Plantability recommendation (bags/hectare) will be used

  • For seeds, the Population recommendation (plants/hectare) will be used

  • When filling in the lot field, if information is available in the industry system, all available indices will be auto-populated

  • Allow entry and editing of the TSW (thousand-seed weight) field

Since a single invoice can include many declared varieties, the header was optimized from three to two lines of basic information entry.

📋 Invoice Declaration Summary

With the changes made to invoice data entry, it was also necessary to update the component that allows auditors to view a summary of entered invoices, with their key data (primarily those used to calculate the crop area to be audited in the field based on the volume declared in the invoice).

The previous version made very simplistic use of collapsible table rows, unnecessarily repeating information and grouping soybeans of the same variety but from different invoices in the same row.

The redesigned view groups varieties by invoice number and declaration type, making it faster for auditors to scan ticket data. It also surfaces a volume summary with the key figures needed for area calculation — no row expansion required.

📈 Results & Learnings

While there are many considerations specific to the soybean royalty collection business model, many of the pain points encountered by users would have been resolved had the original screen design followed good UX practices or been submitted to a heuristic analysis.

As a point for improvement — beyond the items prototyped above — it is worth emphasizing that future initiative timelines must consider user testing as an integral and mandatory part of portal flow redesign projects.

Solution Typology & Effort Framework

Identified problems and opportunities were categorized based on implementation effort, scope, and strategic impact:

System Integration

Challenge: How databases communicate between System 1 and System 2.

"I understood what happened in your case... It's because she [producer] is registered in the system, but it wasn't reflecting here in [System 1], so that's why it wasn't appearing [in the ticket]. But I'll update this for you on my end."

— Support Agent

Core Problem: Divergence between system databases.

Opportunities:

  • Enabler Alignment, standardization, and synchronization of registration data across systems; Data sanitization and synchronization.
  • Enabler Draw on databases and information from other channels (e.g., audits and external bases) to enrich registrations.
  • Structural Definition of governance and Master Data Management (MDM) across agricultural ecosystem cells.

Prototyped Solution (Wireframe): Implementation of Single Sign-On (SSO) login, utilizing data from industry systems to allow quick access via data confirmation, and integrating/sanitizing profile data using the most current system as the "source of truth."

Data Quality & Reliability

Challenge: Reliability of information entered and analyzed from registrations.

"I asked here to receive the password. Only it goes to an email that isn't ours, it's a cooperative email. But the cooperative staff doesn't know this email. We couldn't access it."

— Client

"I can update it here [registration email]. Only I went to put it in your registration and it's reporting that this email is already being used in another registration. So we can't."

— Customer Support Agent

Core Problems:

  • Registration Data: Migrated or legacy data without updates (e.g., missing emails/passwords making access difficult); mandatory email enforcement causing users to create "fake" emails, polluting the database (impacting licensing sign-offs and communication).
  • Behavioral Data: Impact on the efficiency of behavior-based initiatives and intelligence.
  • Lack of Data Completeness: Absence of multiple addresses (used for audits, legal reserve billings) and proper contact tracking numbers. User and operator overload due to repetitive manual data entry.

Opportunities:

  • Quick Win Restructure registration/profile fields.
  • Enabler Validate and complement registration via external bases; enable SMS validation code delivery; in-journey prompts to complete missing data; targeted outreach campaigns to encourage profile updates; regular collection of behavioral data with funnel analysis (Analytics); draw on other channels (audits, ticket documents, customer support contacts) to update systems.
  • Structural Automation rules for inactivating or suspending registrations (e.g., inactivity periods, deaths); pre-defined expiration dates for registrations. Allow email not to be a unique key, triggering internal alerts for repeated usage and identifying partner emails to prevent reuse.

Prototyped Solution (Wireframe): Frontend capability for users to manage and register their best contact details. Backend risk assessment based on unsuccessful contact attempts and profile reliability classification.

Profile & Permissioning

Challenge: Adaptation of Conexão roles to diverse use cases and contexts.

"I don't have a registration, right? Because I'm their [producers'] consultant, right? But the guys have individual, corporate entities... There are several that I manage, right?"

— Client

"Profiles (current or new) must translate the actions the user needs to take in the system seamlessly and with authorization security."

— PM/PO Stakeholder

Core Problems: Current profiles do not reflect the diversity of user contexts. Users with incorrect profiles or unconsidered roles use the system via workarounds (consultants, collaborators sharing logins/hiring credentials, document owners vs. actual users, missing awareness regarding licensing agreements).

Opportunities:

  • Quick Win Dedicated page for partners with FAQs and support links.
  • Enabler Periodic identity proof validations (e.g., app biometrics); structured onboarding and licensing agreement communication; identification of misuse triggers; creation of new profiles for consultants; user logging with access alerts and device limitations.
  • Structural Review of partner data cross-referenced with validated systems; restructuring registration/profile architecture to support multi-profile access across different accounts (even without balance, e.g., employees); defining rules for unregistered users to be added to tickets by partners or farmers.

Permissioning Complexity: Changing permissions is complex, lacks grouping, and consumes technical skill.

Refinement opportunities:

  • Enabler Refinement of existing group permissions.
  • Structural Creation of roles/groups for mass application, user-managed permissioning, and group-wide batch editing.

Prototyped Solution (Wireframe): Collaborative center allowing Distributors and Multipliers to directly register and manage their employees, backed by clearly defined business roles in the backend to prevent risks.

Account Management

Challenge: How users create, edit, and switch between accounts.

"Imagine a group having to have seven different emails, logging into seven different logins to manage royalty balance? It's absurd."

— Client

"38 CNPJs that we have to make purchases for, balances are very separated across all CNPJs. If we could see everything together we would have better control."

— Field Research Participant

Core Problems: Difficulty managing multiple profiles/CNPJs, leading to security issues and login friction; difficulties receiving confirmation or password reset codes; unconfirmed data modifications.

Opportunities:

  • Quick Win Confirmation codes for changes to phone numbers, emails, or documents — validated through customer support.
  • Enabler Facilitated profile switching under the same main ID.

Prototyped Solution (Wireframe): Streamlined account switching component within the top navigation, allowing users to view balances, access registration data, and instantly toggle between associated accounts (e.g., Distributors, Subsidiary CNPJs) without requiring separate login credentials.

ACSSUS (NGO) — Social Impact E-commerce

Postgraduate project (ESPM) proposing a digital sales channel for an Amazonian NGO — integrating UX strategy and financial modeling to create an autonomous revenue stream for artisans in Parintins.

📋 Executive Summary

ACSSUS is a nonprofit founded in 2011 in Parintins, Amazonas, that trains riverside women to produce bio-jewelry using materials sourced from Amazon biodiversity. With nearly all revenue coming from volatile public grants, the organization had only R$ 1,508.25 in available cash at the start of 2026. 📖 Context

ACSSUS (Associação Cidadania, Social e Sustentabilidade) offers vocational training for riverside communities in the Brazilian Amazon, with a focus on women who handcraft bio-jewelry — accessories made from native seeds, fibers, and resins such as tucumã palm, açaí, cipó vine, and paxiúba palm, all harvested non-predatorily.

The organization's cash flow was extremely volatile: revenue came almost entirely from private donations and government funding, which created acute seasonal gaps. Our working hypothesis was that ACSSUS already had a skilled, locally trained workforce and could leverage digital sales to create income that didn't depend on grant cycles.

Starting point: at the beginning of 2026, ACSSUS had only R$ 1,508.25 in available cash after covering fixed operating costs (source: ACSSUS Balance Sheet, base year 2025).

Financial Model

One of this project's key differentiators was integrating financial feasibility into the UX strategy from day one — not as a separate deliverable, but as an active decision-making criterion for scope and prioritization.

Logistics Strategy

Average shipping cost for a single item from Parintins to the Southeast region of Brazil ranges from R$ 77 to R$ 115 — approximately 20% of an individual item's price. To protect margin, we designed a model based on monthly or bi-weekly bulk shipments to a marketplace distribution center in São Paulo, from which orders are fulfilled at a reduced cost across the South and Southeast.

3-Year Projection

  • Projected gross revenue: R$ 831,000

  • Value retained in Parintins (artisan transfers + local hires + operational expenses): ~R$ 539,000

ESG Case for Fundraising

The R$ 49,470 initial investment was framed as a one-time co-sponsorship. ACSSUS has a proven track record with major partners — including Coca-Cola, Esportes da Sorte, and Sebrae — and the digital channel creates a measurable social impact case, with full product traceability and locally generated income, making it a strong ESG opportunity for an investor partner.

Tensions and Critical Decisions

Shipping Costs as the Main Margin Threat

Logistics was the primary risk factor in the model. The decision to use a third-party distribution center in São Paulo was not an assumption — it was the direct result of comparing scenarios numerically. Real shipping benchmarking from Parintins was an essential input to the financial model.

Is a 50% Revenue Share Enough for the Artisans?

Without direct access to the artisans for primary research — a population with limited digital access — it was not possible to validate whether the 50% share covers the actual cost of production in labor hours and materials. This limitation was explicitly documented, with a recommendation to validate before implementation, to ensure the initiative does not inadvertently contribute to the precarious working conditions it aims to address.

Recommended Market Test Before Full Launch

Based on the model, we recommended the NGO start with higher-value items — 3-piece kits at R$ 240 — to map real demand and price flexibility before scaling to the full product mix.
